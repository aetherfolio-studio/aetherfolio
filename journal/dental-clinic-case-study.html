Smile Studio: a dental clinic website that feels calm, clear and easy to book

A concept build for a fictional boutique practice in Midtown Manhattan. How the design, content and booking flow were shaped, and what a real clinic would need.

1. The brief: a phone-first audience

Most people who search for a dentist check the website on their phone, often in a hurry and often a little nervous. The brief was a site for a fictional clinic that answers three questions quickly: what it costs, whether it is gentle, and how to book.

2. What the page had to solve

  • Cost worries: treatments are listed with indicative prices, and the doctor confirms the exact figure in writing after an examination.
  • Trust without proof: a doctor profile, a clinic gallery and placeholder testimonials that a real clinic would replace with its own reviews.
  • Friction to book: one tap to WhatsApp, a short form that opens a pre-filled message, and no account needed.

3. Design direction

The aim was warm editorial luxury rather than the usual clinic-blue template. Cream and deep forest green, with one brass accent. A light serif (Fraunces) carries the headlines and a clean grotesque (Hanken Grotesk) carries the text. Photography sets the mood, so the page has no emoji and no icon set.

Motion is limited to a short rise-in on load and on scroll. It switches off for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion.

4. Page structure

  1. Hero with a WhatsApp booking button and a call button
  2. Three principles: prices first, unhurried appointments, evening and Sunday hours
  3. A numbered list of treatments with indicative prices
  4. A clinic gallery, the rooms and equipment
  5. The doctor, with a short written philosophy
  6. Patient testimonials (placeholders)
  7. Visit: map, opening hours and address
  8. Booking form, then a footer with contact details

5. Build decisions

  • Next.js with Tailwind CSS v4, built as static files. There is no database and no server to maintain.
  • A booking form with no backend. It opens WhatsApp with the patient's name, phone number and concern filled in. Nothing is stored on the site.
  • Photo slots with a tonal fallback. If an image fails to load, the layout still looks finished.
  • Mobile first. The layout was checked at phone and desktop widths.
  • Kept out of search while it is a concept. It is marked noindex so it does not compete with a real clinic's listing.

6. What is still a placeholder

The clinic, doctor, testimonials, prices, address and phone number are all placeholders. The imagery was generated with AI and is labelled as illustrative. A Lighthouse and accessibility audit has not been run yet, so this build's speed and accessibility scores are not measured.

7. What a real clinic version would need

  1. Content from the clinic: the doctor's credentials, real photos, and verified prices and services.
  2. Real patient reviews, with consent, in place of the placeholders.
  3. A Google Business Profile that matches the site's address and hours.
  4. A medical disclaimer and a privacy notice for the booking form.
  5. A booking system, if the clinic wants online scheduling beyond WhatsApp.
  6. Accessibility and speed checks on real phones.

8. Results

This is a concept, so it has no client results yet. Its purpose is to show the approach. Once a real practice uses a version of it, this section should carry that practice's own numbers, such as booking requests per month.
